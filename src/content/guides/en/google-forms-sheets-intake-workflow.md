## Google Forms and Sheets: a simple intake workflow for a small business

Build a lightweight request tracker with clear ownership and basic data safeguards.

> **Important**: Service interfaces and policies can change. Confirm current steps in official documentation.

## Step-by-step solution

### 1. Define the process first

Decide which requests arrive, who handles them, what counts as complete, and how long records are needed. Start with subject, description, contact method, and any necessary privacy notice or consent.

### 2. Create the form

Add only essential questions in Google Forms and explain why information is collected. Check who can respond. Never request passwords or payment credentials. Submit a test response.

### 3. Connect a response spreadsheet

Link responses to Google Sheets. Avoid manually sorting or deleting raw response rows; use a separate working tab or view. Add Status, Owner, Next action, and Last contact columns.

### 4. Agree on simple statuses

Use New, In progress, Waiting for customer, and Closed. Assign responsibility for checking new submissions and avoiding duplicate handling. Validate the manual process before automating.

### 5. Limit access

Share the spreadsheet only with authorized staff. Review editor rights, public links, and notifications. Never publish the response sheet openly.

### 6. Know the limitations

Forms plus Sheets is a lightweight tracker, not a full CRM. It lacks robust row-level permissions, sophisticated auditing, and guaranteed service levels. Sensitive data or larger teams may require a dedicated tool.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] A public form does not require a public response sheet.
- [ ] Collect only the personal data you need.
- [ ] Test with fictitious records before launch.
